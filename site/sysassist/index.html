Where it works in the V

On the left side it drafts and reviews — from customer requirements down to the architecture. On the right side it drafts test cases from what you have approved.

SYS.1

Requirements elicitation

Consolidates stakeholder requirements scattered across documents and drafts clarifying questions where the intent is unclear.

SYS.2

System requirements

Decomposes stakeholder requirements into system requirement drafts with trace links, and checks existing ones for completeness, unambiguity and verifiability.

SYS.3

System architecture

Proposes the allocation of system requirements to architecture elements and flags interfaces that are not yet defined.

SYS.4 · SYS.5

Test case drafts

Drafts test cases from approved requirements and interfaces — preconditions, steps, expected results — each traced back to its source.

Technical questions

Does SysAssist replace a systems engineer?

No. SysAssist prepares, you decide. Every suggestion waits in a review queue — nothing is written to your ALM without an engineer’s approval.

Does it only review, or does it also write requirements?

Both. It decomposes stakeholder requirements into system requirement drafts (SYS.1 → SYS.2), proposes their allocation to architecture elements (SYS.2 → SYS.3), reviews existing requirements and drafts test cases. Everything it drafts waits for your approval.

What if the model is wrong?

You reject the finding with one click and a short rationale. Every finding shows the rule and the evidence it is based on, so it is easy to judge — and the rejection is recorded like any other decision.

Where is the decision history kept?

On the item itself, as comments in your ALM: the finding, the decision and the rationale. There is no separate log to maintain — and in an assessment, the “why” sits right next to the requirement.

Does it create test cases on its own?

It drafts them from approved requirements. A draft stays a draft until you review and approve it — nothing is created in your test management without you.

Where does our data physically live?

Depending on what we agree — integration with an AI engine already present in your organization (e.g. Copilot) or a fully on-premise deployment.
